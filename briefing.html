Hearthlight · Crew briefing

Session Zero: Crew Briefing

Issued October 11, 2026 · from your Guardian, Ixca

Before we begin

This is the briefing for our Session Zero, the evening where we build your characters, set the table's rules, and make a few promises to each other before the story starts. It takes about ten minutes to read. Most of what follows is a conversation, not a lecture.

This game is built so that you never need to read a rulebook. Each of you will build a character from a menu of choices, a tool will do the arithmetic, and the world will explain itself as we go. All you need to bring is curiosity and a willingness to be surprised.

I will play the world, and every person in it. Some of them will be kind to you.

The pitch

Ash is falling on Luthadel, as it has every day since before anyone's grandmother was born. It settles on the rooftops and in the gutters, and it lies gray across the shoulders of every skaa who walks the canal roads before dawn. Out beyond the walls, the sky is the color of old iron. At night the mists rise, thick and white, and the city goes quiet in a way it never does by day.

It is the 720th year of the Final Empire. The Lord Ruler has reigned in Kredik Shaw since before the oldest records begin. The nobles live in Allomantic splendor, and the skaa work and die beneath them. The Steel Ministry reaches into every household, and obligators with their ink-stained tattoos and ledgers see to it that nothing slips by unrecorded. Somewhere in the Ministry's keeping is a long list of names. Anyone whose blood carries the nobles' power, and who is not noble, is a name on that list.

That includes you.

Each of you burns a single metal. You learned it early, and the lesson was a hard one: hide it, always, from everyone. Then someone found you before the Ministry did. A hidden survival network with Terris roots has been quietly pulling skaa Mistings out of harm's way for years. It gave you a place to belong and a reason to keep your head down.

The place is Hearthlight.

To anyone passing, Hearthlight is a chartered staffing guild. The Canton of Resource licenses it to train and place household servants in noble homes. The guildhall is warm, smells of beeswax and wet wool, and the people there are kind. The guild's famous service is the Writ of Clean Blood, a sealed certificate stating that a worker carries no hidden Allomantic heritage. The great houses pay well for it, because they are afraid of spies in their own kitchens.

Your writ says you are clean. It is a lie, and so are the ones your colleagues carry. Hearthlight runs the same search the Ministry runs, looking for gifted skaa. The difference is what it does when it finds one. The Ministry hunts them. Hearthlight shelters them, issues them a false writ, and sends them into the houses where the network needs eyes.

Each of you has a placement now: a household, a role, a persona that you wear more easily every week. You report to the guildhall to collect assignments and pay. You also have a second job, one the guild's license would never allow: the heists, infiltrations, extractions, and quiet violence that Hearthlight can't do by daylight.

You have a Handler, the one who knows your face and your real name, who sends you out into the mists and is waiting when you come back bruised and shaking. You have a Registry where the guild's records are kept by someone quiet and exact, and your lives depend on what's written there.

Records can be trusted, until they can't. Everyone in this city lives inside somebody else's version of the truth. Yours is just better hidden than most.

That's the surface. Scadrial has always had more beneath it, and finding it is the game.

Four promises

These four promises describe the kind of story this is. I am making them to you, and I am asking you to agree to them in return. If any of them sounds like a game you would not enjoy, tell me tonight. I will not take it personally, and we will adjust.

1. The city does not wait for you. Luthadel has its own clock. Houses scheme, obligators audit, and the mists come up whether or not your crew is watching. If you chase a problem, you can change how it ends. If you ignore one, it does not pause politely. It goes on without you, and you live with where it lands.

2. What happens to you is earned, never random. This is a hard world, and some things will go badly. But I will never spring a disaster you had no way to see coming. When something bad happens, you will be able to trace it back to a choice, yours or someone else's, and you will understand how the story got there.

3. Trust can be misplaced, and records are people's work. Someone you rely on can be wrong. A document you were sure of can be wrong. A friend, a ledger, a seal, a promise from the guild: any of them may carry a flaw you cannot yet see. That is not me tricking you. It is the nature of this city, and every deception in this story will be one you could have caught.

4. Attention is rewarded. If you notice things, you will be rewarded for noticing. If you let them slide, that will cost you, sooner or later. This is a game for people who pull on loose threads, and the threads are always there to be pulled.

Two promises to this table

I am making two more promises to the five of you in particular, because I want everyone here to feel safe playing at full tilt.

Your story is yours. The thing your character cares about most, the thread that is theirs alone, belongs to you. Another player's choices can complicate it, delay it, or make it harder. Nobody at this table can end it for you. Only your own character's choices can close that door.

The world can be beaten. This city is dangerous, but it is not unbeatable. Every disaster I put on the clock will have a way out you can see in advance, and often several. You will always have more than one road and more than one attempt. If you lose something, it will be because a door was open and you chose another, not because there was no door.

Lines, veils and the X-card

The Final Empire is a cruel place, and a story set in it will touch some dark things. Oppression is the water this city swims in. Violence happens. Children in the guild's care will sometimes be in danger, and I will write those scenes with care and not for shock. Your characters will also face hard questions about their own conduct, including whether saving a person and using that person can be the same act.

To keep that darkness something we choose together, we will use three tools.

  • Lines are things that will not appear in the game at all. If something is a line for you, say so, and I will keep it out.
  • Veils are things that can happen but stay off the page. We will know they occurred, and we will draw the curtain before the details.
  • The X-card is a signal anyone can use at any time, for any reason, with no explanation needed. Type X in the Discord text channel, or say “pause.” We will skip, soften, or step away from what we were doing, and the story goes on.

You can also tell me privately, by message or in person, at any point in the campaign. I will keep every answer confidential. The darker this story gets, the more important it is that all of you can stop it.

What a session looks like

Most sessions follow the same shape. Your Handler briefs a job, such as a placement to work, a thing to take, or a person to bring out. You prepare, go into the mists, and do the work, with a few scenes of talking and sneaking and now and then a fight. Afterwards you come back to the guildhall and drink tea, and your Handler asks what you did, what it cost, and what you are carrying. A session runs about three hours. You can pass on any question I ask at any point, and the story goes on without you having to explain.

Your crew

The five of you are lateral hires who joined Hearthlight within the same season, each placed in a different household. You eat at the same table at the guildhall and you know each other by sight and by gossip, but none of you has been told what the others are. Tonight is when you decide how far to trust each other, and the story will test that choice.

How we play

You need to know four things, and I will teach them as we go.

  1. You roll a d20 and add a skill. Beat the difficulty and you succeed. A routine task is 10, a hard one is 15 or 20.
  2. Sometimes I will roll the plot die. When the moment matters, I add a special six-sided die. It can bring an Opportunity, a Complication, or nothing. A natural 20 on your d20 always brings an Opportunity, and a natural 1 always brings a Complication. This is how the world answers you.
  3. In a fight, you choose when to act. Act early with two actions, or later with three. That one choice is most of the strategy.
  4. You burn one metal. You are a Misting, so you have a single Allomantic power, and you start with it only partly awake. You will learn it by using it. Nobody has to learn all eight.

Two words to know. A Complication is not a failure. It is the world adding a cost or a twist, and it can always be traced to something already on the table. An Opportunity is the same kind of gift in the other direction. And when I say the city has its own clock, I mean it: between sessions, things move, and at the start of each session I will tell you what you could have seen.

One table normWhen you use an emotional metal, a Rioter's or a Soother's, on another player's character, you ask that player first, and their answer is final. On the people of the city it works, you roll for it, and it has consequences in the story, including with people who have come to trust you. Nobody's character is ever moved against their player's wishes.

There are eight Allomantic metals in the world, and the Crew Dossier, an in-world case file, will lay them out when you build your character. It asks a few questions and suggests a fit. It does the arithmetic, so you can spend the evening on who your character is.

Questions we will ask at the table

On Session Zero night, I will ask each of you three questions, in character, as we walk through the guildhall together. There are no wrong answers, you can pass on any of them, and you can start thinking about them now.

  1. What is a door your character will not walk through? Some choices are impossible for them, whatever the cost.
  2. What do you want more of in this story? Sneaking, fighting, scheming, talking your way past a guard, or something else. I will build the game toward it.
  3. What is one thing your character cares about? A person, a place, a promise, a hope. It can be small. It will matter more than you expect, and I will hold it carefully.
